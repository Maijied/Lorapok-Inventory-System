# Selling

## Before your first sale of the day

Open the shift and count the float — the cash actually in the drawer. If the
count is wrong at open, every figure for the rest of the day is wrong by the
same amount and nobody will know which.

## Ringing up

Scan the barcode, or type the product name or SKU into the box. A USB scanner
types for you; there is nothing to set up. If you have no scanner, **Use
camera instead** works on a phone.

For a handset, the IMEI is captured as part of the sale. That is what lets you
answer "which exact unit did we sell this customer" six months later, when they
come back about a warranty.

## Split payments

A customer paying half cash and half bKash is normal. Add each payment
separately — the sale is not complete until the payments add up to the total.

## Getting a price wrong

You cannot change a price at the till. That is deliberate: it is the lever
used to cover a shortfall, so it belongs with whoever manages the shop. Ask
them to change it in **Products**, then scan again.

## Fixing a completed sale

You cannot delete one. A sale is reversed by recording a return, which puts
the stock back and the money out, and leaves both entries standing. If you
cannot see the return option, you do not have permission for it — that is also
deliberate.

## Closing

Count the drawer again and close the shift. The difference between what the
system expected and what you counted is the thing worth looking at. A small
difference happens; the same difference every day does not.
